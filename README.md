# Cascade Intelligence · urban digital twins of Raleigh and Nagpur

Urban digital twins of Raleigh, North Carolina and Nagpur, Maharashtra, with a crisis-cascade layer
that predicts how a flood, a grid fault, a toxic release or a crowd propagates through drainage,
roads, power, telecom, emergency response and hospitals up to 72 hours ahead. The city models are
real: OpenStreetMap buildings, streets, rivers and facilities rendered in 3D. The cascade
intelligence is one analytical layer on top of them, the way production CIM and digital-twin
platforms are structured.

## Run it

```bash
python3 -m http.server 8000      # from the repository root
# open http://localhost:8000          (Nagpur, the default city)
# open http://localhost:8000/#/?city=raleigh
```

Any static file server works. Opening `index.html` straight from disk (`file://`) also works, but
browsers block local `fetch` there, so the map falls back to live OpenFreeMap tiles.

**Everything you see is a simulation.** The city (facilities, streets, creeks, buildings) is real
OpenStreetMap data; the hazard, every sensor reading, citizen report and outcome are generated by a
modeled scenario. The interface says so: a **Simulation** badge, a **Now / Replay** time mode
instead of "live", the clock marked as scenario time, scenarios tagged *modeled* or *reconstruction*,
and the inspector's feed and physical readings (stage, occupancy, speed…) marked as illustrative,
since they are derived from the risk index rather than measured. A *reconstruction* (Nagpur's
23 Sep 2023 cloudburst) takes the rainfall's timing and totals from reported figures for the real
event; its sensor streams, citizen reports and outcomes are still simulated.

### Two cities

The city is part of the link (`city=nagpur` or `city=raleigh`) and the selector sits next to the
scenario. A link without a city opens Nagpur. Each city brings its own data file (`src/js/data/geodata*.js`), time zone (EDT, IST),
systems and scenarios:

| | Raleigh | Nagpur |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monitored facilities | 44: drainage gauges, a pumping station, junctions, substations, hospitals and EMS, weather | 57: the same systems plus telecom (a telephone exchange and four mobile-site clusters) |
| Scenarios | Tropical remnant rainfall, convective flash flood, heat wave, grid fault and telecom blackout (all modeled) | Monsoon cloudburst · 23 Sep 2023 (reconstruction), chlorine release at the Seminary Hills water treatment plant, immersion-day crowds and an evening storm (modeled, Nagpur only), plus the four generic ones |
| Interventions | A hand-written catalogue for Raleigh's facilities | Generated from the dependency graph: the facilities that feed the most consequential links get the remedies |
| Modeled population | 1,412 cells, about 454,000 residents | 1,790 cells, about 2.94 million residents |

The Nagpur data, its scenarios, the population grid, telecom, the generated intervention catalogue
and the crisis analytics were ported from
[vizsh/72_hours_CASCADE](https://github.com/vizsh/72_hours_CASCADE) (at 9bc9058) with the owner's
permission; this site's interface and renderer are its own.

### Pages and links

The site is a set of pages in one document, addressed by the URL hash, so each engine and the 3D
city are built once and shared, and every view can be linked to.

Six features sit in a permanent sidebar on the left, present on every view including the
3D twin, grouped as **Operations** and **Public**. Each row carries a chip computed from
the engine at the issue time (the city's state, the crisis count, the plan's reduction),
so scrubbing the clock moves the sidebar with the rest of the site. It collapses to an
icon rail, and below 960 px becomes an overlay.

| Page | Path | What it shows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Situation | `#/` | The briefing view of the same 3D twin (same map, timeline and inspector; no second renderer). The asset inventory gives way to an outlook panel with the city's state, a computed briefing, measures (critical now, forecast critical within 24 h, Cascade-R, modeled residents in high-risk areas now and in 24 h), risk by system with the next forecast CRITICAL crossing, and a prioritised list of crises and upcoming crossings. A strip above the map shows the next crossings in predicted order (observed solid, forecast dashed with probability), or "No cascade currently forecast". The camera frames the leading hazard; crisis footprints are on. Selecting an event selects the facility, moves the clock to it, pauses playback and opens the inspector, which links to the crisis, City twin and Response. Collapsible panels (predicted course, population exposure, highest-risk facilities, traffic and access, sensor evidence, the Chronos benchmark) appear only when the engine has data for them. |
| Crises | `#/crises` | A board of linked views, independent of the 3D twin (no map, no renderer): Cascade-R with direct-hazard against through-link stress per system; the learned dependency network between systems or facilities (confirmed, candidate and pruned links, plus direct hazard forcing) with an inspector; a 72 h timeline (Cascade-R, or the selected facility's risk with its forecast 10–90% band, and the predicted threshold crossings); the registry, a system impact matrix (stress flowing now along learned links, system to system, derived from the facility links), critical facilities and recent sensor evidence. One selection drives every view; clicking the timeline moves the shared time; the selected crisis stays in the link (`c=`) for City twin and Response. |
| Crisis | `#/crises/<id>` | One crisis: how it began, its members and what drives each now, the order in which they cross HIGH RISK and CRITICAL (observed, then forecast with probability), the residents near its members (modeled), its history, and links to the board, City twin and Response. |
| City twin | `#/twin` | Free investigation in the same app: the full asset inventory, layers and alerts (collapsed until opened), the system cascade strip and the legend. The 3D operations room, with its own drawers for the learned dependency graph, the intervention planner and replay validation. With `c=<crisis id>` it draws every crisis footprint, frames the camera on that one and shows it in the breadcrumb. |
| Response | `#/plan` | The action catalogue with what each action is worth alone, a crew budget, the planner's recommendation, a before/after ledger (critical facility-hours, hospital and EMS hours, P(a hospital goes critical), facilities reaching critical), what the plan costs elsewhere, and the **decision deadline**. |
| Warnings | `#/warnings` | A plain-text situation report, an OASIS CAP 1.2 alert per crisis with one info block per city language, and the public cell-broadcast text as a handset receives it, with its GSM-7 / UCS-2 encoding and segment count. Everything is marked EXERCISE. |
| What-if | `#/whatif` | Pick a place, an event (heavy rain, earthquake, hospital outbreak, heat wave, toxic gas release, grid failure, crowd surge) and its size; the page draws the chain of effects as a knowledge graph, stage by stage, with each effect's typical onset, likelihood, causes, what helps, and the real facilities, schools and pharmacies nearest the place. Built from a fixed knowledge base (`src/js/engine/whatif.js`) scaled by severity bands (IMD rainfall and heat-wave categories, USGS magnitude classes; the rest are the tool's own); template reasoning, not an engine forecast. It links to the engine's full scenario where one exists. |
| My area | `#/area` | The forecast for one neighbourhood, phone-first. Search 120+ neighbourhood names or use the phone's location. You get a plain verdict with the rain now against the rain coming; how it reaches you (the hazard → drains → roads → power → phones → ambulance → hospital chain for the facilities that serve you, each with its time, likelihood and the learned link that carries it); nearby schools, colleges and pharmacies with whether their access is forecast to be cut; what to do and by when in the city's languages, with a message to share; today's real weather from Open-Meteo; the next 36 h per system; and roads to avoid. |

**The decision deadline** is what a dashboard cannot show. `interventions.deadline(k, ids)`
re-runs the plan with the crews starting 0, 2, 4 … 24 h later, still deciding at `k` on
information up to `k`. Nagpur's chlorine release decided at T+31 is worth 55% fewer
critical facility-hours if the crews move now, 36% after two hours and nothing after six;
the monsoon plan decided at T+37 keeps 80% of its value until T+47. Same engine, same
page, opposite decisions.

**Segment counts** in the warnings are the same kind of honesty. GSM 03.38 packs 160
characters into one SMS; Devanagari forces UCS-2 and 70, so the Nagpur flood warning is
one message in English, two in Marathi and three in Hindi.

The shared context travels in the query (`src/js/site/store.js`):

| Parameter | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `city` | `raleigh` or `nagpur`; absent = the city in use (Nagpur on a fresh load) | `city=raleigh` |
| `s` | scenario id; absent = the city's first scenario | `s=flash` |
| `t` | view time in hours from T+0; absent = follow the simulated now | `t=40` |
| `m` | `replay` for replay mode; absent = now | `m=replay` |
| `a` | selected facility id | `a=HSP-WMR` |
| `c` | crisis shown on the twin | `c=CR-RAL-4573` |
| `area` | neighbourhood shown on My area | `area=Dharampeth` |

For example `http://localhost:8000/#/twin?city=raleigh&s=flash&t=40&a=HSP-WMR` opens Raleigh's
flash-flood scenario at T+40 with WakeMed selected, and
`http://localhost:8000/#/crises/CR-NAG-5627?city=nagpur&s=monsoon2023&t=37` opens the Nagpur flood
crisis as issued at T+37. Selecting a facility, switching mode, scenario or page adds a history
entry (back/forward replay them); scrubbing time updates the current one. Unknown scenarios or
facilities are dropped from the link, and unknown pages fall back to the overview with a note.

The pages read what was known at the issue time: a link with a time after the simulated now shows
the now. While another page is in front the twin is paused (its clock and render loop hold still),
and its time, selection and crisis follow the context when you come back. A city switch on the
twin reloads the page (map bounds, tiles and meshes are per city); the other pages switch in place.

`python3 tools/build_dist.py` produces `dist/index.html`, a single page with all CSS and JavaScript
inlined, plus `dist/data/bundles/`: the tiles and glyphs packed as base64 JSON for hosts that only
serve text and web media types, and `dist/data/models/`: the hero models as base64 JSON with their
texture images. It runs under a strict Content-Security-Policy (`connect-src 'self'`, scripts only
from cdnjs and cdn.jsdelivr.net) and is what the published artifact is built from.

### Renderer: Mapbox GL JS or MapLibre GL JS

The map code is written against the Mapbox GL JS API (`Map`, `Marker`, `fill-extrusion`, `flyTo`,
`addSource`/`addLayer`, canvas sources, custom layers).

- **MapLibre GL JS 4.7 (default):** the open-source continuation of Mapbox GL JS with the same
  API. It renders the OpenStreetMap vector tiles shipped in `data/tiles` through a `twin://`
  protocol, so the twin works offline and inside sandboxed frames that block third-party requests.
  This is the path the realism work below was built and tested on.
- **Mapbox GL JS v3:** set `mapboxToken` in `config.js` to a Mapbox public token. The twin then
  loads the Mapbox Standard style with Mapbox's own 3D buildings, landmarks and trees, pulls its
  palette toward the dark command-center look (`setConfigProperty` on the `basemap` import) and
  switches its light preset (dawn, day, dusk, night) as the timeline is scrubbed. The hero models
  go into Standard's `middle` slot and the dependency arcs and radar into `top`. If the token is
  rejected or the style does not load within 15 s, the failed map is removed and the app falls
  back to MapLibre.
  **Untested:** no valid token was available while this was built. Only the fallback has run (an
  invalid token gets a 401 on the style and the app comes up on MapLibre); Standard itself, the
  `setConfigProperty` keys, the light preset switching and the slot placement have not.
  **Known limitation:** Standard's buildings come from Mapbox's own tiles and cannot be clipped,
  so at a hero site the Mapbox building and the hero model overlap. The OSM footprint swap
  described below only works on MapLibre.

## Realism pipeline (MapLibre)

The city is drawn in three layers that share one sun, computed from the simulation clock
(`core.sunPosition`, NOAA low-precision ephemeris). T+0 is Sat 24 Oct 2026 00:00 local time and
the scenario's now is T+34.5, unless the scenario has its own clock: the Nagpur cloudburst
reconstruction starts at Thu 21 Sep 2023 06:00 IST with its now at T+37.
Scrubbing the timeline moves the sun, the sky, the haze and every shadow.

1. **City buildings** (`src/js/map/buildings.js`): a WebGL2 custom layer that decodes the building
   layer of every z13+ tile MapLibre loads and meshes it: walls and flat roofs from the footprint
   (earcut), gable roofs fitted to the oriented bounding box of small houses. A procedural shader
   draws facades in world metres per building class (punched windows, ribbon glazing, curtain
   walls, storefronts, ribbed industrial cladding), roofing (shingle courses, membrane, rooftop
   plant), sky reflections and sun glints in glass, and lit windows at night that fade to a dim
   average once they are smaller than a couple of pixels. Each wall carries its direction in its
   vertex data, so the window grid stays exact up close. A sun shadow map (4096², 2048² on
   software rasterisers) is re-rendered only when the sun, the shadow region or the drawn tiles
   change, and shades the buildings and a ground quad. Without WebGL2 or local tiles, or if the
   driver rejects its shaders, it steps aside and the style's fill-extrusions show.
2. **Hero models** (`src/js/map/heroes.js`): a Three.js layer (three@0.160.0, imported from
   cdn.jsdelivr.net on first use) that shows a detailed glTF model of the selected facility, with a
   ground ring and halo in its risk colour, sun-matched lighting and its own shadows. The OSM
   footprints under each hero site are lifted out of the tiles (`geodata.sites`) and hidden while
   the model stands there, in both the city-building layer and the fill-extrusion fallback. Models
   exist for hospitals, EMS stations, the pumping station, substations, traffic sites and the
   weather station (on its roof at 13.5 m); stream and culvert gauges keep their marker only.
3. **Basemap and sky** (`src/js/map/style.js`): the dark CIM palette, a sky and horizon haze per
   day phase, and the MapLibre light following the sun.

Configuration (`config.js`): `cityBuildings: false` keeps the fill-extrusions, `buildingShadows:
false` drops the shadow map, `shadowSize` overrides its size.

Frame times downtown (1600×900, zoom 16.4, pitch 60, about 250k triangles; `node tools/test/perf.js
<url> [gpu]`; medians over several runs on a laptop, so the ranges are mostly machine load):

| | City buildings + shadows | City buildings, no shadows | Fill-extrusion fallback |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Intel integrated GPU (D3D11) | 25–55 ms | 25–45 ms | 16–36 ms |
| SwiftShader (headless, software) | 0.84–1.15 s | 0.82–1.0 s | 0.49–0.81 s |

On a GPU the shadow pass costs little: it runs once per camera move or sun step (0 times across
static frames, `stats.shadowPasses`), not every frame.

### Provenance and honesty notes

- **Building classes and heights are estimates.** `tools/enrich_buildings.py` classifies each
  building by the OSM land-use polygon its centroid falls in and, where OSM has no height tag (most
  of both cities), infers one from footprint area and class (`inferred = 1` in the tiles). Explicit
  OSM heights are kept. Facade style, window rhythm and roof shape follow from those estimates, not
  from surveys or imagery.
- **Nagpur uses the same hero models.** They are generic facility types, so Government Medical
  College gets the hospital model and its OSM footprints are lifted out the same way; they are
  not replicas of the Nagpur buildings.
- **The hero models are authored, not downloaded.** No realistic, CC0-licensed models of these
  facilities (or of generic ones at the needed detail) could be downloaded, so they were built at
  real scale in code (`tools/models/facility_models.js`). They are representative of each facility
  type, not replicas of the real buildings. Two sites overlap a neighbouring OSM building that was
  not lifted out of the tiles: the north edge of the Duke Raleigh Hospital model and one corner of
  the Lake Boone pumping station yard.
- **Textures:** Poly Haven PBR sets (CC0): asphalt_02, bitumen, brick_wall_10, concrete_floor_02,
  concrete_pavement, concrete_slab_wall, concrete_wall_008, corrugated_iron, factory_brick, gravel,
  leafy_grass, painted_metal_shutter, red_brick_03, sparse_grass, white_plaster_02. The `gen_*.png`
  decals (helipad, emergency and EMS signs, chain-link) are drawn by the build page.

### Rebuilding the models

```bash
node tools/models/export_models.js preview/   # needs Playwright + Chromium and cdn.jsdelivr.net
```

It opens `tools/models/build.html` in headless Chromium, builds each model with three.js, writes
`data/models/*.glb` (and optional preview renders), then `externalize.py` points the glTF images at
the shared files in `data/models/textures/`. `tools/build_dist.py` wraps them as base64 JSON for
single-page builds (`modelBundles`).

## How the intelligence layer meets the brief

| Requirement | Implementation |
| --- | --- |
| **Multi-horizon forecasting** | 200-member ensemble. Each facility gets P(critical) and a fan chart at +1/6/12/24/48 h, plus time-to-critical with p10–p90. Forecasts are perturbation forecasts anchored to the current analysis. |
| **Dependency discovery** | No structure is given. 71 candidate links (7 deliberately spurious) are learned online by sparse Bayesian regression (ARD) on the sensor streams, then confirmed, pruned or left as awaiting evidence. By T+72 h on the tropical storm: 32 confirmed, 12 pruned. A separate raw-stream screen (lagged correlation, weather partialled out by distributed-lag regression) checks the structure at system level. |
| **Cascade prediction** | Lagged propagation with feedback loops: hospital → EMS → hospital, diversion between hospitals, load transfer between substations. The timeline, cascade strip and graph show the stage-by-stage sequence, observed and forecast, numbered in propagation order. |
| **Concurrent crises** | `analytics.js` (ported from vizsh/72_hours_CASCADE with permission) groups stressed facilities into crises over the learned dependency graph: each gets an ID, hazard family, lifecycle stage (precursor, emerging, escalating, active, recovering), member systems and districts, the next facility forecast to go critical, and coupling or contention with other crises. **Cascade-R** compares the stress arriving through dependencies with the stress the hazard applies directly; above 1 the cascade is feeding itself. Both are computed now and as forecasts. The Crises pages show the registry, each crisis and its history; the twin draws their footprints (650 m around each member). Residents in a footprint come from the modeled population grid (`population.js`: each cell counts toward its nearest monitored facility within 1.5 km), the same model the twin's population layer uses. |
| **Online learning under drift** | A localized particle filter with forgetting (about a 23 h memory) and adaptive tempering keeps the effective sample size at or above 12%. Recursive estimators update every 30 minutes without retraining. "Trigger cascade" replays the streams from T+0 so you can watch this happen. |
| **Uncertainty and adversity** | Sensor trust comes from spread-normalized residuals and flatline detection. Suspect channels are quarantined, meaning their past evidence is forgotten quickly. In the tropical run the frozen gauge, offline camera and biased rain gauge are flagged with no false flags. Planted false citizen reports score 12–16% corroboration; genuine ones score 84–96%. |
| **Causal explainability** | Every risk value decomposes into baseline, hazard and each upstream link (posterior weight ± sd, lag, mechanism, upstream sensor reading and trust). The strongest chain back to the hazard is shown, e.g. Rainfall → Pigeon House Branch → Capital Blvd → EMS Station 2 → WakeMed. |
| **Constrained counterfactuals** | 7 actions with crew costs and side effects (an evacuation loads I-440; a detour loads Wake Forest Rd). They are re-simulated from the decision time on the most probable members, and a greedy planner chooses within the budget. At T+34.5 h with 5 crews it picks pumps, EMS staging and an early Capital Blvd closure: −13% critical facility-hours, −30% hospital/EMS critical hours. |
| **Generalization** | Seven scenarios across two cities run through the same engine without retuning: tropical rainfall, a convective flash flood, a heat wave with a derecho and a grid fault with a telecom blackout in either city, and in Nagpur a monsoon cloudburst reconstruction, a chlorine release (Gaussian plume) and immersion-day crowds. The learned structure changes with them. |
| **Temporal validation** | Historical mode replays the window. Every forecast uses only data up to its time stamp and is scored against the hidden truth. |

## Machine learning: pretrained models

Two pretrained models sit beside the engine. Neither changes it: the engine's output is
byte-identical with or without them.

**A second opinion on every forecast: Chronos-Bolt** (`amazon/chronos-bolt-small`, Apache-2.0). A
time-series foundation model pretrained on public data, used zero-shot: it has never been trained
on these cities. Offline, `tools/ml/chronos_forecast.py` gives it each facility's sensor stream as
observed up to every hourly issue time (with the planted noise, frozen gauges and dropouts, and
nothing after the issue time). It forecasts the next 24 h as nine quantiles. P(CRITICAL within h)
is the largest per-step probability of the stream reaching CRITICAL, read from those quantiles.
The results ship as `src/js/data/chronos.js` (250 KB, every scenario of both cities).

`src/js/engine/second.js` sets it beside the ensemble. It recomputes the ensemble's probability
exactly as `engine.validation()` does (`second_probe.js` checks the two agree in every run) and
scores both with the same code on the same cases. The Situation page shows where the two disagree
now, with the replay scores. The crisis page lists both probabilities for every member.

| Scenario | Brier, ensemble | Brier, Chronos | Warned in time (ens / Chronos) | False warnings (ens / Chronos) | Median lead (ens / Chronos) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Raleigh · tropical | 0.077 | 0.127 | 14 / 13 of 17 | 4 / 6 | 18.5 h / 1 h |
| Raleigh · flash flood | 0.071 | 0.143 | 17 / 12 of 22 | 1 / 6 | 15.5 h / 0.5 h |
| Raleigh · heat wave | 0.019 | 0.071 | 10 / 12 of 13 | 0 / 3 | 22.5 h / 1 h |
| Raleigh · grid fault | 0.017 | 0.053 | 7 / 2 of 8 | 1 / 3 | 16.5 h / 0 h |
| Nagpur · monsoon 2023 | 0.041 | 0.154 | 29 / 23 of 34 | 0 / 7 | 22 h / 0.5 h |
| Nagpur · tropical | 0.060 | 0.146 | 25 / 22 of 27 | 4 / 8 | 20.5 h / 1 h |
| Nagpur · flash flood | 0.079 | 0.161 | 24 / 14 of 32 | 0 / 2 | 15 h / 0 h |
| Nagpur · heat wave | 0.023 | 0.088 | 17 / 15 of 18 | 0 / 5 | 18 h / 0 h |
| Nagpur · grid fault | 0.017 | 0.039 | 4 / 6 of 8 | 0 / 5 | 21 h / 5 h |
| Nagpur · festival crowds | 0.022 | 0.040 | 2 / 6 of 6 | 0 / 17 | 22 h / 0.5 h |

Scored hourly from T+1 on every facility, for CRITICAL within 6, 12 and 24 h (8,700 cases on the
Nagpur cloudburst). A warning is P(CRITICAL within 24 h) of 60% or more. In the chlorine release
nothing reaches CRITICAL. The ensemble wins every run, and the lead times say why. Chronos sees
only each facility's own stream, not the weather forecast or the dependency links, so it can follow
a rise but not see one coming. Where it catches more events (heat, grid fault, crowds), it does so
by warning often and late. Like the table above, these scores use simulated ground truth.

**Search by meaning: all-MiniLM-L6-v2** (`Xenova/all-MiniLM-L6-v2` through Transformers.js 3.0.2,
Apache-2.0, about 23 MB). The field at the top of the sidebar (`/` to focus) embeds the query and
every page, facility, area, scenario and current crisis, then ranks them by cosine similarity. "Where
will the power go out" finds the substations and the grid-fault scenario; "chemical leak" finds the
chlorine release. The model downloads from Hugging Face on the first search and is cached by the
browser. Until then, or where it cannot load (offline, or the single-page build, whose
content-security policy only allows its own origin), results come from word overlap and say so
(`src/js/site/search.js`).

```bash
pip install chronos-forecasting                     # CPU is enough: all 11 runs take a few minutes
node tools/ml/export_streams.js                     # sensor streams → tools/ml/cache/ (git-ignored)
python tools/ml/chronos_forecast.py                 # → src/js/data/chronos.js
node tools/test/second_probe.js [--city nagpur]     # exactness check and the scores above
```

### Data pipeline

```bash
python3 tools/fetch_tiles.py data/tiles             # OpenFreeMap tiles for the extent (already committed)
python3 tools/build_data.py                         # decode tiles → src/js/data/geodata.js
python3 tools/build_data.py --city nagpur           # → src/js/data/geodata_nagpur.js
python3 tools/enrich_buildings.py --city nagpur     # building classes, heights, hero-site lifting
python3 tools/build_places.py                       # neighbourhoods, schools, colleges, pharmacies → src/js/data/places.js
```

`tools/spec.py` (Raleigh) and `tools/spec_nagpur.py` are the authored knowledge bases: which
facilities are monitored, which links engineers consider possible, the city's time zone and
overview camera, and the sensor faults planted in its streams. `tools/mvt.py` is a dependency-free vector-tile decoder.
`build_data.py` places gauges at exact creek × road crossings, routes links on the street and
waterway graphs, chunks the corridor and drainage networks, and computes the substation service
areas.

`build_places.py` reads the `place` and `poi` layers of the same tiles for My area: Nagpur has 88
named neighbourhoods and suburbs (plus the 30 monitored districts), 83 schools, 37 colleges and 45
pharmacies; Raleigh 35, 120, 26 and 22. They live apart from the geodata, so the engine never sees
them.

### My area: what is computed and what is real

- **Rain now** is the scenario's storm at that spot (what a radar would show). **Rain coming** is
  each ensemble member's storm there over 24 h, weighted by how well the member matches the
  evidence. The figures are the median of each member's own total and wettest hour, plus a
  1-in-10 high case.
- **How it reaches you** lists the first HIGH RISK crossing (observed, or forecast with its
  probability) of every facility within 2.2 km, plus your nearest hospital and ambulance station.
  "Because" names a learned dependency from an earlier step; otherwise the hazard that drives it.
- **A school's access** is read from the nearest monitored road or river crossing within 1.2 km,
  so it is modeled, and the page says so.
- **Today's real weather** is a live call to Open-Meteo's GFS ensemble (31 runs) for that
  spot. It is real and current, and it does not feed the simulation. The single-page build's
  content-security policy blocks it, and the page says so.

```bash
python3 tools/test/csp_server.py 8766             # serve dist/ under a strict CSP
```

The browser tests launch Chromium on SwiftShader (software GL), where a frame of the city takes
close to a second; `interactions.js` can time out on its phone step there, with or without the
city-building renderer. On a machine with a GPU, drop the SwiftShader flags. On Windows,
`python -m http.server` occasionally refuses one of the page's parallel script requests (its
listen backlog is 5), which leaves the page on its loading screen; reload or use another server.
The twin tests (smoke, interactions, phone, heroes, perf, shot) are written against Raleigh's
facilities and open `#/twin?city=raleigh` when the URL has no hash. In Git Bash, set `MSYS_NO_PATHCONV=1` before
passing a URL that contains `#/…`, or the shell rewrites it as a Windows path.
