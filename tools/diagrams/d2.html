Data flow — from a sensor in the street to a decision deadline

Six stages. Every instrument in the city is reduced to one thing: a risk stream per facility, sampled every thirty minutes. Everything downstream works on that stream and nothing else, which is why the field layer can be swapped between a simulator and real telemetry without touching the model.

Citizen reports — the deliberately unreliable channel
Public submissions and social posts, geotagged. Treated as evidence with a trust weight, never as ground truth: each city's data carries planted false reports (for example “Explosion at Mankapur substation” at T+12.5) which the system must not act on.

Sampling and transport
Each source polled or pushed at its own rate, timestamped at source, carried over whatever the utility already runs — MQTT, REST, SFTP drop, or an operator typing it in.

2 · Ingest — reduce everything to one shapeThe only interface the model has.
The contract
For every facility i and every half-hour step k: one number in [0,1] meaning how far this asset is from failing, plus a timestamp and a quality flag. Nothing else crosses this line.
obs[k · N + i] → Float32Array
Normalise
A metre of river stage, an ampere of motor current and a 40% bed occupancy are mapped onto the same scale by each asset's own capacity and threshold, set in tools/spec*.py.
Resample & align
Sources arriving at 1 min, 5 min and hourly are aligned to the 30-minute grid. Late arrivals are accepted; the step is recomputed.

Trust, estimated online — not configured
Each stream carries a trust score from its own EWMA one-step-ahead residual and a flatline test. A stream the model keeps being surprised by is down-weighted automatically, and its forecast widens instead of breaking. A 42% trust score is never displayed as a 42% physical reading.

Where the prototype actually sits today

Stage 1 is not connected. In place of the field layer, scenarios.js generates the same streams synthetically — with the noise, the stuck gauges, the dropouts and the false reports included — for 27 scenarios across two cities. Everything from stage 2 rightward is unchanged by that substitution, which is the whole reason the boundary is drawn where it is.

What connecting it for real would take

One adapter per source that emits {facility, timestamp, value, quality} onto the stage-2 contract. The hard parts are institutional, not technical: data-sharing agreements across the discom, the water board, traffic police and the hospital network — the same boundaries that make the cascade invisible in the first place.
